BIOLOGICAL FARMING

What is Organic Farming?

Organic farming, also known as Biological farming, focuses on locally grown products and large-scale use of local resources. It avoids the need for synthetic fertilizers, relying instead on natural methods of crop production and management. It is also called “Organic Agriculture”.

The organic farming mainly relies upon:

Crop rotations
Use of crop residues
Animal manures
Legumes
Green manures
Off-farm organic wastes
Bio fertilizers
Mechanical cultivation
Mineral-bearing rocks
Control of insects, weeds, and other pests

Advantages

• Helps maintain environmental health by reducing pollution.
• Reduces human and animal health hazards from chemical residues.
• Keeps agricultural production at a sustainable level.
• Reduces cost of production and improves soil health.
• Ensures optimum use of natural resources and conserves them for the future.
• Saves energy for both animals and machines, and reduces risk of crop failure.
• Improves soil physical properties like granulation, tilth, aeration, and water-holding capacity.
• Improves soil chemical properties by retaining nutrients and promoting favorable chemical reactions.
